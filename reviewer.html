2. What you are being asked to judge

Index tuners usually rank candidate indexes by the optimizer's estimated cost. Wu, Dutt, Xu, Narasayya, and Chaudhuri (PACMMOD / SIGMOD 2025) show that the cheapest estimated plan is often slower when you actually run it. About 11% of indexes from Azure SQL auto-indexing had to be reverted for that reason. The paper's number, not ours.

The honest check is to build each candidate and time the workload. That is too expensive on a large database. The project asks whether a sample of the database can stand in for that check.

The decision to preserve is the execution-time winner, not the optimizer's favourite. A configuration is a set of indexes for the whole workload. Ground truth is a real CREATE INDEX plus measured query time. HypoPG is only a baseline source of candidates. LLMs are the other source.

Two questions:

  1. Does the configuration that wins on the sample D' also win on the full database D?
  2. Which way of building D' is cheapest for a given quality of that match?

Closest published system: SQL:Trek (Lightstone and Wang, PVLDB 2025). It executes indexes on one 5% per-table uniform sample. It does not compare sample designs, and it does not report Kendall tau or top-1 against a full-database ranking of the same configurations.

4. The experiment

This is the protocol Srinivas sent on 5 October 2026. The earlier per-query ranking of single HypoPG indexes is not the answer to it.

1Generate configurations (sets of indexes), including stronger models.
→
2On the full database: index build time and total query runtime.
→
3That ranking is ground truth.
→
4Build D'.
→
5Same workload on D'.
→
6Kendall tau, top-1, evaluation cost reduction.

Advisors see the schema, existing indexes, and the 99 query texts. They do not see the data and do not run SQL. One Gemini run was thrown out because it had opened earlier result files. The file in results/advisors/gemini-3-8-flash.json is the clean rerun.

build_configs.py drops unknown columns, tables under 1000 rows, indexes that already exist, and duplicates. Result: 33 configurations in results/configs_sf1.json.

Two scores

Score A (primary): sum of the 99 query medians. This is the execution-time ranking the proposal cares about.

Score B (secondary): index build time plus score A. Srinivas listed both ingredients. They are scored as a second ranking, not mixed into one number without saying so.

Lower is better on both. A query that errors or hits the 20 s cap is recorded as 20 s, the same on D and on D'.

Comparison metrics

MetricMeaning
Kendall tau-bAgreement of the two orderings over the 33 configurations. 1 is identical order, 0 is none. 95% interval from 1000 bootstrap resamples of those 33.
Top-1Does D' pick the same configuration that D ranks first? Exact, not a tie rule.
RegretFull-database score of D''s pick, divided by the best full-database score. 1.000 is perfect. 1.014 is 1.4% slower on D.
Top-3Is D's winner among D''s three fastest?
Cost reductionSum over configurations of (build + workload) on D, divided by the same on D'. Optional column adds the time to build the sample.

6. How a configuration is timed

These rules exist because their absence produced a wrong Q72 result that was later retracted (cache, re-ANALYZE, autovacuum). See docs/lab-notes.md.

  1. Autovacuum is off cluster-wide.
  2. ANALYZE once per database, then frozen. Re-ANALYZE redraws the planner's sample. Every result row stores an md5 of pg_stats.
  3. One configuration at a time: create all its indexes (timed), run the 99 queries, drop them. Leftover ix_c% indexes from a killed run will silently change the next candidate merge, so they are dropped at the start of an eval.
  4. Per query: one discarded warm-up, then the median of the timed runs, 20 s cap.
  5. statement_timeout does not reliably cancel a query stuck in disk read on this machine. The harness records the cap when it finally sees the cancel, not wall-clock hours.

Do not ANALYZE tpcds_sf1 again. That would change the ground-truth fingerprint (91bd9a3118e7556f).

7. Sampling methods

TPC-DS is a snowflake. If you sample two tables independently at rate p, an equality join keeps about p² of the original pairs. At 5% that is 0.25%. Join-aware samplers exist to avoid that. Math and diagrams: protocol.html.

CodeNameWhat it doesRan at 5%
b0BernoulliEach row of each table kept independently. Floor 20k rows, cap 1M at 5% (SQL:Trek sizing).own stats, and with full stats copied on
sysPageSame size rule, TABLESAMPLE SYSTEM (whole pages).own stats
uitemUniverseKeep a fact row iff hash(item_sk) falls in the kept fraction, same hash in all facts. Dimensions in full.own stats, and with full stats copied on
fkFK closureBernoulli on the three sales facts. Keep matching returns. Inventory thinned among sampled items. Dimensions in full.own stats
stratStratifiedOn sales, keep at least 5 rows per sold-date, then Bernoulli. Other facts as b0. Dimensions in full.own stats
c4Stats transplantSame rows. pg_statistic and reltuples/relpages copied from the full database. Index stats are not copied. Do not ANALYZE after.Bernoulli and universe only
C5Workload-awareKeep rows the queries actually touch.not built

Universe, FK, and stratified keep dimensions in full, so they stay large. Bernoulli and page shrink every table. That is why cost reduction is 40x to 90x on the latter and about 3x on the former.

All numbers below are score A (sum of 99 query times) unless noted. 33 configurations. One seed. 20 s cap counted as 20 s.

Full database

No extra index: 190.7 s. Best: Grok's small dimension filters, 172.9 s (build 0.63 s). Worst of the 33 is the empty configuration. Spread: 17.7 s.

9. What was expected, what happened

ExpectationResult
A 5% sample can name a near-best configurationPartly. Exact top-1 on 1 of 5 native samples. The common miss is 1.4% slower, and the true best is in the top 3 of every native sample.
Join-aware samples beat independent random on TPC-DSNo, at this scale. Tau is similar or worse, and they are only ~3x cheaper because dimensions stay full.
Giving the sample the full database's statistics helps (SQL:Trek future work)No, on the two samples tried. Tau fell. Universe regret rose to 7.8%.
The sample ranking should look like the full rankingOnly to within noise. The full database vs itself is tau 0.45. Bernoulli 0.51 and page 0.46 sit on that ceiling.
The claim that is actually supported. On a memory-resident SF=1 TPC-DS database, with these 33 configurations, a 5% Bernoulli or page sample evaluates ~25x to 90x cheaper and picks a configuration within 1.4% of the full-database winner. Join-consistent sampling and statistics transplant did not improve that. The ranking below first and second place is not stable even on the full database.
The claim that is not supported. That small samples reliably recover the execution-time-optimal configuration on a large, disk-bound analytical database. SF=1 is the wrong physical regime for that, the cap hides the slow queries, and the configurations barely separate.

10. Threats and deviations from the proposal

  1. Wrong scale and engine. Assignment: SQL Server, 100 GB. Here: PostgreSQL, 1 GB in cache. SF=10 (~27 GB) is IO-bound on this laptop; timeouts do not cancel stuck reads.
  2. The 20 s cap. Seven queries never leave the cap. Any index that would have helped those queries is invisible. The ranking is a contest among the fast queries.
  3. Small spread. 17.7 s on a ~190 s total. Overnight the machine moved 5.3 s. Configurations are near-ties.
  4. n = 33, one seed, one rate. Intervals are wide. 1% and 10% were not run. Workload-aware sampling was not built.
  5. C4 is incomplete. Statistics copy only on Bernoulli and universe.
  6. Advisor quality. If every candidate is within a few percent of no index, sample/full agreement does not prove sampling works. It proves there was little to get wrong. Grok and Opus are the only sources that moved the needle much.
  7. Retracted and superseded work. Early Q72 timings were wrong (cache and stats). Early per-query SF=1 metrics (top-1 0.27 to 0.40) are not this protocol. Do not cite them as the result.

11. Questions worth asking

These are the points a first review can usefully push on. They are not a list of known bugs.

  1. Is SF=1 an acceptable iteration, or must the next number be SF=10 on a 64 to 128 GB machine before any sampler is ranked?
  2. Should the 20 s cap be raised, or should the seven always-capped queries be reported separately so they stop padding every total with 140 s?
  3. Is 1.4% regret a success when the best-to-worst spread is only 9%?
  4. Score A or score B as the headline? They disagree on stratified top-1.
  5. Was HypoPG as a baseline fair, given it was ranked by cost and then timed, while the LLMs never saw the data?
  6. Is the stats-transplant failure a finding, or an artifact of copying table stats without index stats?
  7. Should more seeds at 5% be run on this laptop, or is that polishing a noisy ranking?

Suggested next measurement, if the winner must sit above the noise: two full rankings at SF=10 on a machine where the 27 GB database is not stuck on disk, then Bernoulli and page at 1/5/10% plus one join-consistent sampler, each with native stats and with transplanted stats, one extra seed. Until that exists, more SF=1 cells will not settle the original question.
